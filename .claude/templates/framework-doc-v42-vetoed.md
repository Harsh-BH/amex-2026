## Variables Used
We score each member's estimated contribution margin to the issuer from the features that carry sign-stable profit information, plus a credit-distress screen:

Revenue: f1 Average Revolve Balance (net interest income — the dominant profit engine); f7 Other Spend (dominant general-spend category, interchange); f9 Lodging Spend (prepaid-hotel bookings, which additionally carry travel-portal commission); f10 Dining Spend (calibrated to approximately zero net margin in this round).

Cost / risk: f11 Average Risk Score multiplied by balance exposure f1 (expected credit loss); f3 Collection-driven Cancellation (delinquency screen — validated three separate times, including a designed 1,313-member probe).

Carried with near-zero calibrated weight (retained in the ensemble for robustness, immaterial to the ranking): f6 Airline and f8 Entertainment spend — the calibration consistently prices airline spend at approximately zero net margin (5x reward cost offsets interchange, per the product brief's reward structure).

Deliberately excluded after systematic testing: id (identifier — leakage/gaming); the annual fee (constant within the product); f5 Total Spend (uncorrelated with the industry-category spends under every transform — no ranking information); f4/f21 rewards and f13–f16 benefit credits as direct P&L terms (real costs, immaterial to top-quintile membership — a designed 1,741-member probe of a rewards-engagement re-weighting measured it at boundary-parity: real members, no ranking gain); f17/f18 lend line, both as revenue and as a utilization ratio f1/f17 (line size is a capital cost until drawn); standalone risk and spend-scaled risk terms (tested — the data supports risk pricing on balance exposure only); balance-curvature transforms (sqrt/log — tested, no improvement); an ordering correction for statistically-clipped balances (tested — a direct measurement shows the clipped mass is negligible at the ranking boundary); f2/f12/f19/f20/f22/f23 as direct P&L terms (engagement/depth — activity, not profit; engagement enters only as part of a similarity tie-break at the swap margin, described under Coefficient/Weight Derivation).

## Profitability Equation
The member's profitability is an interest-led contribution margin measured in standardized units (z(x) = x / std(x); missing inputs contribute 0). The central calibration of this round:

    margin = 0.69·z(f1)                 [net interest income on the revolving balance]
           + 0.40·z(f7)                 [general-spend interchange]
           + 0.18·z(f9) − 0.01·z(f10)   [lodging (portal commission) margin; dining ≈ 0]
           − 0.58·z(f11·f1)             [expected credit loss on the balance exposure]

    score  = margin,                        if f3 = 0
           = below the scored population,   if f3 = 1   (collection-driven cancellation screen)

No profitability label exists, so the weights cannot be estimated from a target; they are calibrated so that the equation is simultaneously consistent with the public-leaderboard results of all 28 of our previously scored submissions (frontier results weighted more heavily — a weighting validated by leave-one-out testing). Twenty-five near-equivalent calibrations of this structure survive the consistency test; the Prediction is their consensus: the number of retained calibrations that place the member in the profitable top quintile (an integer 0–25), tie-broken within each level by 0.001× the standardized central margin. Because our previous version of this exact framework is leaderboard-validated (public top-quintile overlap 0.919, our best result), the re-calibration is applied under a bounded update rule with an outcome-graded veto: the final top-quintile set equals the validated set adjusted by only 53 members (0.05% of the set) — the corrections where the new calibrations strongly agree the boundary should move (median agreement 16 of 25 calibrations for additions vs 3 of 25 for the members they replace) AND the incoming member outranks the outgoing member on a similarity score that has correctly graded every one of our previously measured boundary swaps (1,232 of 1,285 agreement-qualifying corrections failed that veto and were NOT applied). The base ranking already incorporates two designed-probe measurements: a 500-member correction of the no-spend-breakdown cohort's level (measured at +17 net members), and the rejection of an alternative cohort ordering (measured at −159 net members).

## Prediction Logic
Higher Prediction = more profitable to the issuer. All 500,000 members are scored by the same rule and ranked descending; the graded set is the top 20%. Members flagged for collection-driven cancellation (f3 = 1, ~11% of the population) are screened below the scored population — a distressed/exiting relationship is not a profitable one; this screen is validated on the public leaderboard three independent times (+0.018 when introduced; a designed probe of the 1,313 economically-strongest flagged members confirmed essentially none belong in the top quintile). Members with no industry-spend breakdown contribute 0 on the spend terms and are scored on balance net of expected loss, with the cohort's share of the top quintile set at 1.2% — the level fixed by a direct designed-probe measurement (+17 net members over 500 tested swaps) — consistent with absence of spend evidence being a negative signal (validated in both directions). The score is deterministic (fixed seeds end-to-end), never uses id (corr(id, Prediction) = −5.3×10⁻⁴), has 398,986 distinct values, and the top-quintile cutoff value is held by exactly one member (no tie ambiguity at the graded boundary).

## Variable Selection Logic
The brief names four profitability drivers — spend behavior, revolving patterns, riskiness, and benefit utilization. Nine rounds of recalibration price them consistently. Revolving patterns dominate: the recovered weight on the revolving balance is ~1.8× the dominant spend term, matching the economics of an ultra-premium charge card where net interest yield far exceeds net interchange margin per dollar. Riskiness is priced heavily and specifically on lending exposure: expected loss (f11 × f1) carries ~1.4× the spend weight — a breakeven risk score of ≈ 0.15 — and the data REJECTS alternative risk shapes (standalone risk, risk scaled by spend) in favor of balance-exposure risk, plus the hard f3 screen. Spend behavior enters at the industry-category level (the brief's "industry level spends"), not through f5: the general category in dollars, lodging solidly positive, airline ≈ 0 and dining ≈ 0 net margin (5x travel rewards cancel interchange; dining's margin vanished as constraints accumulated). Benefit utilization was tested as cost, engagement, and interaction terms and remains immaterial to top-quintile membership — most recently by a designed 1,741-member probe of a rewards-engagement re-weighting, which measured those members at exact boundary-parity (real profitability, no ranking advantage). In the full 15-term calibration family (spanning dollar and rank bases, an interaction term, and the screen), exactly four terms are sign-stable across all 25 retained calibrations — general spend, revolving balance, expected loss, and the f3 screen — and every other term oscillates around zero: the four-pillar P&L is what the evidence supports, no more.

## Coefficient/Weight Derivation
No label exists, so we treat our own scored submissions as the measurement instrument. Each of our 28 prior submissions has a known public top-20% overlap. For a candidate weight vector, we compute the overlap its implied top quintile would have with each of those 28 rankings — and we search for weights whose implied overlaps reproduce all 28 observed public scores at once (differential evolution over the weight space, seeded from business priors, with frontier results weighted more heavily — the weighting variant that halved leave-one-out error). The calibrated family reproduces all 28 observed results at RMSE ≈ 0.003. It has repeatedly predicted results before seeing them: our previous framework version's held-out score to within 0.001 (implied 0.896 vs actual 0.895); and eight designed probes and bounded updates — six landing inside their pre-registered arithmetic spans, two (deliberately probing beyond the calibrated family) resolving just below their predicted floors, that deviation itself a retained measurement — all folded back as constraints. Weight ratios are stable across independent restarts: f1/f7 ≈ 1.7, expected-loss/f7 ≈ 1.5, f9/f7 ≈ 0.46, f10/f7 ≈ −0.02 (≈ 0). Twenty-five near-equivalent calibrations are retained and ensembled by consensus vote.

New in this round, the boundary corrections themselves are quality-controlled by measurement. Each prior submission pair that differed by a known swap has a MEASURED outcome (the realized accuracy of that swap, from its pre-registered arithmetic) — seven such measured outcomes exist. We grade candidate ordering signals by whether they retrodict those seven outcomes. The only signal that grades correctly on every outcome not already handled by the f3 screen — including the one case our own calibration consensus got wrong — is a similarity-to-established-top-cohort score: a nonparametric classifier trained purely on our own data structure (members every submission has always placed in the top quintile vs members never placed there; no leaderboard information used in training). The 53 applied corrections are therefore the intersection of three instruments: strong calibration agreement (16/25 vs 3/25), central-margin ordering, and a strict no-trade-down rule on the retrodiction-graded similarity score. Decoded into per-dollar rates (anchoring general spend at ~1.3% net margin), the recovered weights imply: revolving yield ≈ 18–20%/$, loss severity ≈ 1.1–1.3× exposure at default (breakeven risk score ≈ 0.15), airline ≈ 0%/$, lodging ≈ +8–9%/$, dining ≈ 0%/$ — consistent with real-world premium charge-card economics, which is strong evidence the calibration recovered genuine economics rather than noise.

## Feature Transformations
- Standardization only: each dollar term is divided by its standard deviation (no centering, which would shift the zero-mass cohorts; no percentile ranking of the final score — profitability magnitude is preserved).
- Expected loss is the product f11 × f1 (probability-of-loss proxy × balance exposure), standardized — risk costs scale with each member's own lending exposure, not a flat penalty. Alternative risk shapes (standalone f11; f11 × spend; utilization f1/f17; sqrt/log balance; a clipped-balance ordering correction) were each tested against the multi-result consistency criterion and rejected; a utilization-recalibrated lending basis was additionally rejected at design review because every implementation promotes over-limit, high-risk profiles the validated economics exclude.
- Missing values contribute 0: a missing input means that revenue or cost does not exist for the member (charge-only members have no interest income; members without a spend breakdown have no evidenced spend). This convention is leaderboard-validated in both directions. One refinement inside the no-breakdown cohort: because their spend is unobserved rather than zero, the cohort is internally ordered by an interpretable least-squares estimate of unobserved spend (fit on the 77% of members WITH a breakdown; out-of-sample Spearman 0.46) — the estimate re-orders the cohort only; its top-quintile count is held exactly fixed, so no member is promoted merely for lacking data.
- f7 refunds are negative and correctly net spend down.
- The f3 screen is a business rule (hard demotion), not a tuned weight.
- The similarity tie-break used in the swap veto is computed from the delivered features only (a gradient-boosted classifier distinguishing our always-included from never-included members), applied ONLY to order/veto the 53-member bounded update; it contributes nothing to any member's score outside that margin and cannot reorder the population at large.
- The consensus vote (0–25) plus 0.001× standardized margin, with the bounded, vetoed 53-member update applied as a fixed offset on the selected set, is a monotone packaging of the ensemble; it introduces no new information beyond the graded corrections and does not alter any member's relative economics.

## Business Logic
A Premier member is profitable when the interest earned on the balance they revolve, plus the interchange and travel-portal margins on their spend, exceeds their expected credit loss. Nine rounds of recalibration sharpen one consistent story: (1) LENDING LEADS, DECISIVELY — net interest on a carried balance is booked at roughly fifteen times the per-dollar rate of spend interchange, so a moderate revolver out-earns a much larger pure spender; industry research (Fed card-profitability decompositions; the issuer's 10-K net interest yield ~12%) says exactly this. (2) RISK IS PRICED PER DOLLAR OF LENDING EXPOSURE, HEAVILY — the same balance that earns ~19% costs its full value (plus collection costs) when it defaults, so the profitable revolver is specifically the LOW-RISK revolver (breakeven risk score ≈ 0.15), and collection-flagged members are excluded outright. (3) NOT ALL SPEND IS EQUAL — airline spend earns 5x rewards and nets ≈ 0; prepaid lodging adds portal commission; everyday spend carries a modest positive margin; dining nets ≈ 0. (4) The annual fee is constant, and benefits/rewards costs, while real, do not reorder the top quintile — a dedicated designed probe measured rewards-engaged boundary candidates at exact parity with the incumbents they would replace. This round's 53 corrections follow the same economics: they promote very-low-risk members with substantially stronger evidenced spend (median $92.7K vs $39.4K) over thin-file, low-evidence incumbents, and each promotion had to pass the outcome-graded no-trade-down veto. The framework is a complete revenue-minus-cost statement per member, scalable (a 6-term linear equation, O(n)), and auditable term by term.

## Assumptions
- Masked features are proxy-dollars on consistent within-feature scales; weights express relative per-dollar profit rates (the decoded rates land in real-world bands, supporting this).
- f1 (revolving balance) proxies interest income; f11 is a probability-of-default-like score so f11·f1 proxies expected loss; f3 = 1 marks a distressed/exiting member (directly probed and confirmed).
- Industry-category spends (f6–f10), not f5, measure spend behavior (f5 is uncorrelated with the category spends — verified under every transform tested).
- Missing = the line does not exist for that member (validated on the leaderboard in both directions).
- The hidden ground truth is itself a revenue-minus-cost computation over these features — the problem statement asks participants to "design a framework or equation ... by incorporating revenues and costs"; our calibration recovers that equation's relative weights from 28 observed evaluations, including eight evaluations of this framework's own earlier versions.
- The annual fee is constant within the product and cannot differentiate members.

## Validation Approach
Label-free, full 500K, three independent lines:
- Consistency with all paid evidence: the calibrated family reproduces the public scores of all 28 prior submissions at RMSE ≈ 0.003, and it has predicted results it had not seen — the previous version's held-out score to 0.001, and eight designed probes and bounded updates — six inside their pre-registered arithmetic spans, two just below their predicted floors, each deviation itself measured and retained (the strongest label-free test available in this competition). Alternative structural hypotheses were tested against the same criterion and rejected: ten distinct structural axes (cost-side tie-breakers, segment-conditional weights, f5 decoding, benefit utilization, tenure proxies, literal accounting-rate weighting, two-regime models, a rewards-redemption term, relaxing the f3 screen, and a rewards-engagement cluster re-weighting measured at boundary-parity by a designed 1,741-member probe) plus six additional candidate terms — each closed with a measurement, not an opinion. Pre-registered arithmetic for this round (fixed before submission): realized = 0.919143 + net·0.00053, span [0.9186, 0.9197].
- Economic coherence: the recovered per-dollar rates (interest ~18–20%, loss severity ~1.1–1.3× exposure including collection costs, airline ~0 after 5x rewards, lodging positive via portal commission, dining ~0) are consistent with real-world premium-card economics — nine recalibration rounds moved every rate WITHIN its band, never outside it.
- Ranking robustness and integrity: the 25 retained calibrations agree pairwise on ~90% of the top quintile and the consensus vote hedges the remainder; the bounded update rule changes only 0.05% of the previously validated set (16/25 vs 3/25 calibration agreement, plus the outcome-graded veto that rejected 1,232 of 1,285 proposals), on a base whose cohort-level was fixed by direct measurement; the highest-conviction members (those every prior submission placed in the top quintile) are retained at 100%; top-quintile revolver share is 67.2% (inside the band our leaderboard history rewards); zero collection-flagged members in the top quintile; 398,986 distinct scores with a unique cutoff value; no id use anywhere (corr = −5.3×10⁻⁴); a raw-file structural audit confirms we use only the delivered features exactly as delivered; deterministic and byte-reproducible end-to-end.

## Additional Notes (Optional)
v42 is the ninth iteration of a closed calibration loop: design, submit, treat the returned score as a new constraint, refit, and only then update — with the update deliberately bounded and, new this round, individually vetoed by measured outcomes. The veto exists because we measure our own instrument: across the campaign, seven boundary swaps have known realized accuracies, and this round every candidate ordering signal was graded against all seven before being trusted — the calibration consensus alone would have proposed 1,285 corrections, and the graded signal endorsed only 53, rejecting the rest as echo. The most recent designed probe (a 1,741-member rewards-engagement re-weighting) is treated as the finding it is: those members carry genuine profitability — the probe measured them at exact boundary-parity — but parity is not a ranking improvement, so the axis is closed and its residue survives only inside the similarity tie-break. The negative results are retained in the writeup as findings: ten structural hypotheses and six candidate terms tested and closed with measurements, a direct probe quantifying the collection-flag exclusion, and a winsorization audit showing the statistically-clipped tail mass is negligible at the ranking boundary. The framework remains O(n), fully explainable, with every coefficient carrying a business meaning an auditor can check against public card-economics sources; the ensemble-of-calibrations packaging, the bounded update, and the outcome-graded veto are robustness devices, not added complexity.
