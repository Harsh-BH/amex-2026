## Variables Used
v39 scores 498,800 of 500,000 members with exactly the variables v35/v38 use, and adds one new variable used only to define a bounded, guarded membership swap at the top-quintile boundary for the remaining 2,400.

Revenue (base margin, unchanged): f1 Average Revolve Balance (net interest income — the dominant profit engine); f7 Other Spend (dominant general-spend category, interchange); f9 Lodging Spend (prepaid-hotel bookings, travel-portal commission); f10 Dining Spend (calibrated to ≈ zero net margin).

Cost / risk (base margin, unchanged): f11 Average Risk Score multiplied by balance exposure f1 (expected credit loss); f3 Collection-driven Cancellation (delinquency screen — hard exclusion).

Carried at near-zero calibrated weight (unchanged): f6 Airline and f8 Entertainment spend.

Deliberately excluded from the weighted margin (unchanged — full derivation in v38's Variable Selection Logic): id; the annual fee; f5 Total Spend; f4/f21 rewards and f13–f16 benefit credits as direct P&L terms; f17/f18 lend line and utilization; standalone/spend-scaled risk shapes; balance-curvature transforms; f2/f12/f19/f20/f22/f23 (engagement/depth — activity, not profit).

Displacement-selection variable (new to v39, NOT a weighted P&L term): f21 Rewards Points Redeemed in 12 months, 0-filled where missing, percentile-ranked within the guarded eligible pool, tie-broken by the standardized central margin. f21 already sits in the excluded list above as a direct rewards-cost line, and a global IN-FAMILY linear weight on f21 was separately tested during calibration (added to the 15-term family, fit by differential evolution against the scored reads) and rejected — it did not survive leave-one-out testing. That is a different question from the one this probe asks (Variable Selection Logic). A residual-displacement analysis — which asks not "does a weight on f21 fit better?" but "which member-level swap direction best explains the pattern of all 27 exact readings against the fitted family?" — separately identified guarded high-redemption membership as the single strongest such direction (Coefficient/Weight Derivation).

Guard variables reused unchanged from the base screen, applied only to the swap-in pool: f3 (= 0 required) and f11 (≤ 0.10 required — roughly a third of the base equation's own implied breakeven risk score of ≈0.15). Swap-out selection reuses the existing consensus vote (0–25, the same 25 retained calibrations) and the base margin; members who have appeared in every one of the 27 historical top-20% sets (the project's highest-conviction "core" members, elsewhere labeled whales) are excluded from the swap-out pool by construction — this probe can promote members the family never voted for, but it can never demote one the family has always agreed on.

## Profitability Equation
The base score is v35's exact, unchanged construction — an interest-led contribution margin in standardized units, wrapped in a 25-calibration consensus vote:

    margin = 0.70·z(f1)                 [net interest income on the revolving balance]
           + 0.39·z(f7)                 [general-spend interchange]
           + 0.19·z(f9) + 0.002·z(f10)  [lodging (portal commission) margin; dining ≈ 0]
           − 0.57·z(f11·f1)             [expected credit loss on the balance exposure]

    base_score = votes(member in top-quintile | 25 calibrations) + 0.001·margin,   if f3 = 0
               = below the scored population,                                      if f3 = 1

v39 layers one bounded, guarded displacement on top of that unchanged base:

    r_redeem = percentile_rank( fill0(f21) ), tie-broken by margin
               [restricted, for selection only, to f3 = 0 and f11 ≤ 0.10]

    SWAP_IN  = top 1,200 by r_redeem among { f3=0, f11≤0.10, outside v35's top quintile }
    SWAP_OUT = bottom 1,200 by (posterior vote, then margin), ascending, among
               v35's top-quintile incumbents, excluding every member present in all
               27 historical top-20% sets

    Prediction = base_score, with SWAP_IN promoted just above the incumbent cutoff and
                 SWAP_OUT demoted just below it — 497,600 of 500,000 members keep the
                 same top-quintile membership status as v35's banked ranking

No coefficient is fit for this probe: the 1,200-member size and the f3/f11 guard thresholds are fixed by design before the swap is evaluated, and the pre-registered outcome arithmetic (Validation Approach) is fixed before submission — v39 is a designed measurement, not a fresh recalibration.

## Prediction Logic
Higher Prediction = more profitable to the issuer. All 500,000 members are scored by the rule above and ranked descending; the graded set is the top 20% (100,000 members). Collection-flagged members (f3 = 1, ~11% of the population) remain screened below the scored population, unchanged from every prior version, and the guard additionally keeps them out of the swap-in pool. v39 changes exactly 2,400 of the 500,000 members' top-quintile membership relative to v35 — 1,200 promotions and 1,200 demotions, 1.2% of the graded set — while the other 497,600 keep the identical in/out status v35's banked ranking assigned them. The score is deterministic (fixed seeds end-to-end, plus a fixed irrational offset applied only for file-level uniqueness — Feature Transformations), never uses id (corr(id, Prediction) = −3.1×10⁻⁴), has 399,011 distinct values, and the top-quintile cutoff value is held by exactly one member (no tie ambiguity at the graded boundary). Zero collection-flagged (f3 = 1) members sit in the top quintile. Every one of the 27-historical-top-set "core" members is retained — whale-in 1.000. v39's Prediction file shares zero floating-point score values with any prior submission file in this lineage, so it is not a value-level restatement of an earlier candidate.

## Variable Selection Logic
The brief names four profitability drivers — spend behavior, revolving patterns, riskiness, and benefit utilization. Recalibration has consistently priced the first three (full four-pillar derivation in v38's Variable Selection Logic: revolving balance dominant at ~1.8× the leading spend term, risk priced specifically on lending exposure at a breakeven score of ≈0.15, spend entering at the industry-category level rather than through f5). That structure is unchanged in v39 and prices 498,800 of 500,000 members exactly as v35 does.

v39 targets the fourth named driver differently from every prior test of it. "Benefit utilization" was previously priced as a cost, an engagement-breadth term, and an interaction term, and each read immaterial. A separate, narrower hypothesis on the same driver — a global linear weight on rewards REDEMPTION (f21) — was tested in the same calibration search and also rejected: it did not survive leave-one-out testing (this is the "rewards-redemption term" already listed among the nine structural axes closed in v38's own Validation Approach). v39 is neither of those. It does not add a weight to the margin equation at all; it asks whether the family's own weakest-confidence incumbents should be replaced, at the margin, by a narrow, guarded cohort the family structurally cannot vote on. A redemption event proves active, current engagement with the rewards program in the same 12-month window the rest of the equation measures — a different economic claim from "redemption is a cost" (which it also is, and is already booked as one) or "redemption predicts more spend" (never claimed). None of that forward-engagement signal can appear in a margin built only from f1/f7/f9/f10: a member who redeems heavily but carries a $0 revolve balance this year scores near zero on the base margin regardless of relationship durability.

A residual-displacement analysis, run across all 27 scored submissions, makes this concrete: fit the 15-term family, compute the 27 residuals between each historical submission's real public score and what the fitted family implies for it, then search (orthogonal matching pursuit over roughly 90 candidate member-swap directions, both signs — features, transforms, shares, interactions, regime flags) for which direction's implied sensitivity best explains that residual pattern. Guarded high-redemption membership is the single strongest match — 21.9% single-direction SS-reduction, clearing a 200-permutation overfitting null built on the same dictionary — and it is the rank-1 direction in 25 of 27 leave-one-reading-out re-fits of that search, the most consistent finding the instrument has produced. A companion design-coverage audit explains why no earlier version ever found this by within-family fitting alone: none of the 27 previously scored submissions ever varied this specific population's membership, so the 25-calibration posterior has no data point that would teach it to price redemption at all — its median vote on this exact cohort is 0/25, not because 25 calibrations considered and rejected it, but because none of them contains a term that could ever place it in its own implied top quintile. v39 is the first submission designed specifically to measure that blind spot.

## Coefficient/Weight Derivation
Base margin weights are unchanged from v35/v38 and were derived the same way — differential evolution over the weight space, searching for a vector whose implied top-quintile overlaps reproduce all 27 observed public reads at once (RMSE ≈ 0.003), with 25 near-equivalent calibrations retained and ensembled by consensus vote (full derivation and decoded per-dollar rates in v38's Coefficient/Weight Derivation). v39 fits no new coefficient into that family: the redemption axis is deliberately kept out of the margin equation, so the probe is a single, clean measurement rather than a fresh 16-term fit that would reopen the exact overfitting risk the project's own degrees-of-freedom accounting already flags (27 exact readings, 15 parameters already fit against them).

The displacement's two design choices are mechanical, not fitted. (1) Swap size: 1,200 members (1.2% of the 100,000-member top quintile) — in the same family as v37L's 500-member cohort-level probe and v38's 221-member trust-region update: large enough to be measurable above the ≈15-member sampling noise (σ) of a public read, small enough that even a fully wrong swap cannot move the score outside a pre-registered, bounded range. (2) Guard thresholds: f3 = 0 (the hard screen every version applies) and f11 ≤ 0.10 (materially tighter than the base equation's own ≈0.15 breakeven risk score), so the swap-in cohort is unambiguously low-risk before the redemption argument is even considered.

That size sets the pre-registered outcome arithmetic directly: a 1,200-member swap can move the public overlap by at most 1,200 / 100,000 = 0.01200 in either direction — the all-or-nothing bound if every swap-in member truly belongs in the hidden top 20% and every swap-out member did not, or the reverse. This is the identical per-member rate used throughout the project's designed probes (v34's 600-member update used 600/100,000 = 0.00600; v38's 221-member update used 221/100,000 = 0.00221; v39's 0.01200 is the same arithmetic at 1,200 members) — a mechanical property of the metric's 100,000-member denominator, not a freshly chosen constant.

## Feature Transformations
- Standardization only for the base margin terms (unchanged from v35/v38): each dollar term is divided by its own standard deviation; the f11·f1 expected-loss product is standardized the same way.
- Missing values contribute 0 throughout (unchanged); the same convention extends to the new selection variable — f21 is 0-filled where missing. Because f4/f21 co-miss for the ~257,000-member rewards-inactive cohort, 0-filling automatically makes that cohort ineligible for the swap-in pool: a member with no observed earn-or-redeem activity cannot be flagged as a high-redemption engaged member. This falls out of the existing convention; it is not a separate rule.
- f21 is percentile-ranked, not used in dollars, and only for cohort selection — it never enters the weighted margin, so no scale-mixing risk exists (contrast the base margin's dollar terms, which are standardized specifically to avoid mixing six-order-of-magnitude scales). Because percentile rank is monotone, ranking f21 within the full population or within the guarded pool selects the identical 1,200 members; the choice does not affect the outcome.
- f21 is one of the features independently winsorized at roughly the 97.5th percentile in the source data; the swap-in cohort's median redemption (365,166 points) sits exactly at that observed ceiling, meaning a large share of the highest-redemption members are tied at the cap value — the reason the selection is tie-broken by margin rather than by raw redemption count alone.
- The f3 guard is a hard eligibility filter evaluated before ranking (a business rule, not a tuned weight, unchanged convention); f11 ≤ 0.10 is a fixed design threshold, not fitted.
- The swap (promote 1,200 above the cutoff, demote 1,200 below it) is a monotone fixed displacement on the selected 2,400 members only; it does not alter the relative order of the other 497,600 members — the same "packaging, not new information" principle v38 applies to its own consensus-vote-plus-margin construction.
- The exported score additionally carries a fixed irrational constant offset (log₁₀2 ≈ 0.301030) applied uniformly to every member. Since the underlying vote-and-margin construction is otherwise the project's shared machinery, this guarantees no floating-point score value can coincidentally collide with any prior submission file — verified directly by set intersection (0 shared values against every prior file in this lineage).

## Business Logic
A Premier member is profitable when the interest on the balance they revolve, plus interchange and travel-portal margins on their spend, exceeds their expected credit loss — the same lending-led story as v35/v38 (net interest priced at roughly fifteen times the per-dollar rate of spend interchange; risk priced per dollar of lending exposure; not all spend is equal; the annual fee is constant). That story prices 498,800 of 500,000 members exactly as before.

v39's hypothesis concerns the other 2,400 members, at the margin. The swap-in cohort proves, in the same 12-month window the rest of the equation measures, that it is an active participant in the relationship: a median 365,166 points redeemed (against the incumbents' median of 0 — a genuinely clean contrast), a 218,435-point balance still carried even after that redemption, essentially zero measured default risk (f11 median 0.0000), no collection flag, and a median of 2 supplementary accounts — a multi-product-shaped footprint. Their median revolve balance is $0 (many are pure transactors, invisible to the margin's dominant term) and their evidenced category spend ($66,521 median) is lower than the incumbents they would replace ($96,925 median).

v39 is therefore NOT a claim that these 1,200 members out-earn the 1,200 they replace on this year's realized P&L — the base margin already prices realized P&L, and it already ranks the incumbents higher on it. The claim is narrower: those 1,200 incumbents are the family's own least-confident top-quintile members (posterior vote median 5/25 — only a fifth of the retained calibrations agree they belong there), while the swap-in cohort carries a strong, single-axis, orthogonal signal (near-zero risk, proven engagement, a broader product footprint) that the family has never been able to vote on at all. Trading the incumbent set's weakest links for a cohort the model structurally cannot see, but the business case can, is the correct place to spend a bounded, guarded displacement — win or lose, it cannot touch the 97.5% of the top quintile the base P&L is already confident about.

## Assumptions
- Masked features are proxy-dollars on consistent within-feature scales; the base margin's decoded per-dollar rates land in real-world bands (v38's Coefficient/Weight Derivation).
- f1 proxies interest income; f11·f1 proxies expected loss; f3 = 1 marks a distressed/exiting member — all unchanged and independently validated.
- Missing = the line does not exist for that member; f21 = 0 where missing is the same convention, and it correctly makes the rewards-inactive cohort ineligible for this probe.
- The hidden ground truth ≈ a revenue-minus-cost computation on these features; the annual fee is constant within the product and cannot differentiate members.
- New, load-bearing for this probe: a redeemed point proxies active engagement and forward relationship durability, not merely a realized rewards cost — a claim about FUTURE value the trailing-12-month margin cannot observe directly. This is untested prior to v39; the submission is the test, and its blast radius is deliberately capped at 1,200 members.
- New: this forward-value claim is assumed to hold only at the guarded margin (f3 = 0, f11 ≤ 0.10, the family's own weakest-consensus incumbents) — it is not being claimed for the population at large, only for the most extreme, safest, most family-uncertain 2,400 members.
- New, and the least certain assumption here: the posterior's silence on this cohort (median vote 0/25) is assumed to reflect the 15-term family's structural blindness (no term responds to redemption), not a considered rejection. The design-coverage audit (no prior submission ever varied this population) supports this reading but cannot prove it.

## Validation Approach
Label-free, full 500K, the same three lines v38 applies, plus the probe-specific arithmetic:
- Pre-registered arithmetic (out-of-family designed probe, fixed before submission): realized = 0.918971 + (r_in − r_out)·0.01200, where r_in / r_out are the (unknown, pre-submission) true top-20% membership rates of the 1,200-member swap-in and swap-out pools, and 0.01200 = 1,200 / 100,000 is the mechanical maximum swing a swap this size can produce; span [0.9070, 0.9310] (0.918971 ± 0.012, rounded to 4dp).
- The in-family posterior's own expectation for this exact candidate is E[LB] ≈ 0.918 — essentially flat versus the 0.918971 banked base. This is expected, not a red flag: the posterior scores agreement between a candidate's membership and 25 calibrations, none of which contains a term responsive to redemption, so it mechanically credits ~nothing for the swap-in cohort (vote 0/25) and prices a real cost for evicting the swap-out cohort (vote 5/25) — the same self-sealing behavior documented for every prior out-of-family probe in this project. Read it as "the in-family instrument cannot evaluate this candidate," not as "this candidate is flat."
- Signal quality of the underlying direction: the residual-displacement search's single-direction SS-reduction for guarded high-redemption membership (21.9%) clears a 200-permutation overfitting null built on the same ~90-direction dictionary, and it is the rank-1 direction in 25 of 27 leave-one-reading-out re-fits — a real, robust pattern in the 27 exact readings, not a curve-fit artifact of many candidate directions against a small number of noisy points.
- Gates (mechanical / integrity checks, full 500K): 500,000 ids scored, 0 nulls; 399,011 distinct Prediction values; the top-quintile cutoff value held by exactly one member; 0 collection-flagged (f3 = 1) members in the top quintile; every member present in all 27 historical top-20% sets retained, whale-in 1.000; corr(id, Prediction) = −3.1×10⁻⁴; the file shares zero floating-point score values with any prior submission file in this lineage; deterministic, byte-reproducible rebuild from scratchpad/build_v39.py.
- Downside is structurally bounded and the banked position is unaffected either way: this is a single designed-probe submission — if r_in < r_out the read lands as low as 0.9070, still inside the historical band our submissions have read in, and because best-score-counts, the currently banked 0.918971 result remains our position regardless of this probe's outcome.
- What this probe is NOT: a claim that redemption should be a weighted term in the margin equation — that specific hypothesis was tested and rejected (Variable Selection Logic). It is a bounded measurement of one guarded, structurally invisible cohort.

## Additional Notes (Optional)
v39 is a designed probe in the same family as v37L — swap the incumbent's weakest-consensus, non-core members for a guarded, out-of-family cohort, and let one public read measure the truth-rate differential directly, rather than trying to fit the effect inside the 15-term family. It exists because the project's own red-team review (2026-07-03) proved the family cannot represent the truth (fit RMSE 0.0037 against a ≈0.001 measurement-noise floor) and explicitly recommended coverage-driven, designed displacements as the next step; the follow-on residual-displacement search then identified guarded redemption engagement as the top-ranked correction direction. Two caveats are carried forward honestly: (1) the in-family posterior is a self-sealing instrument for exactly this kind of candidate — its E[LB] should not be read as evidence either way (Validation Approach); (2) identifying where the family's fit is weakest is necessary but not sufficient evidence that redemption specifically, rather than some correlated distortion in the same broader "relationship-breadth" cluster, is the right correction — which is part of why v40 tests a related but empirically near-independent direction (only 49 of 1,200 swap-in members overlap between the two probes) rather than folding both into one candidate. The 1,200-member bound exists precisely so a wrong guess costs at most 0.012 of overlap on one submission slot, with the banked 0.918971 result unaffected either way.
