## Variables Used
v41 scores 496,518 of 500,000 members with exactly the base P&L variables v35/v38/v39/v40 use, and — for the first time — folds four rewards/engagement variables directly into the calibrated family that decides the remaining 3,482 members' top-quintile membership at the boundary.

Revenue (base margin, unchanged): f1 Average Revolve Balance (net interest income — the dominant profit engine); f7 Other Spend (dominant general-spend category, interchange); f9 Lodging Spend (prepaid-hotel bookings, travel-portal commission); f10 Dining Spend (calibrated to ≈ zero net margin).

Cost / risk (base margin, unchanged): f11 Average Risk Score multiplied by balance exposure f1 (expected credit loss); f3 Collection-driven Cancellation (delinquency screen — hard exclusion).

Carried at near-zero calibrated weight (unchanged): f6 Airline and f8 Entertainment spend.

NEW this round — the engagement cluster, now calibrated rather than heuristic: f21 Rewards Points Redeemed (12 months), f4 Rewards Points Balance, and f19 Number of Supplementary Accounts (all standardized, z-scored, 0-filled where missing), plus f22 Emails Opened (last 6 months, percentile-ranked, then standardized — it is a small-integer count, not a dollar amount). These four are fit jointly, not one at a time, as an extension of the 15-term base family, and it is the joint block that clears the family's own internal consistency bar: three of the four (f21, f4, f22) are positive with 100% sign agreement across every retained calibration; f19 is the one ambiguous term (75% sign agreement) carried for ensemble robustness rather than a confident individual read. f20 (Active Charge Cards) and f23 (Emails Clicked) were tested alongside these four in the same extension work but are not part of the retained joint cluster and remain excluded.

This is a genuinely new role for f21/f4/f19/f22: earlier rounds tested f21 as a direct rewards-cost dollar line (rejected) and, separately, as a single heuristic rank-selection variable for a bounded swap (v39) that did not survive an in-family leave-one-out test as a lone linear weight; f19 (paired with f20) was tested only as a heuristic rank-selection variable (v40). Neither of those single-variable tests is what changed here — the joint four-term ADDITION to the fitted family, at small calibrated weights, is this round's central, and different, finding.

Deliberately excluded from the weighted margin (unchanged from every prior round): id; the annual fee; f5 Total Spend; f13–f16 benefit credits as direct P&L terms; f17/f18 lend line and utilization; standalone/spend-scaled risk shapes; balance-curvature transforms; f2 Cancellation Calls, f12 Logins, f20 Active Charge Cards, f23 Emails Clicked (activity, not profit). f4 and f21 remain excluded specifically as DIRECT dollar cost/liability line items — v41 adds no rewards-cost or points-liability dollar term to the margin — and f19 remains excluded as a fee/capacity revenue term; all four (with f22) enter the score only through the small, standardized engagement-cluster weights described above, a different economic claim (active engagement, not realized dollars) from the ones already tested and rejected.

## Profitability Equation
The base score is v35's exact, unchanged margin, wrapped in a consensus vote — but the vote is now cast by an EXTENDED calibration family:

    margin = 0.70·z(f1)                 [net interest income on the revolving balance]
           + 0.39·z(f7)                 [general-spend interchange]
           + 0.19·z(f9) + 0.002·z(f10)  [lodging (portal commission) margin; dining ≈ 0]
           − 0.57·z(f11·f1)             [expected credit loss on the balance exposure]

           (unchanged since v35 — used here only as the within-group tie-break, "margin" below)

Extended family (the 15-term base family plus the four engagement terms), fit fresh against all 27 scored submissions by the same instrument used every prior round. Sixteen seeds are fit; the four whose RMSE sits within 1.5× of the best (0.0016) are retained. Each of those four is a full weight vector — medians shown below, not a single averaged equation:

    core (unchanged sign, 100% agreement across all 4 retained calibrations):
      +0.549·z(f1)  [interest]            +0.311·z(f7)  [spend]
      −0.331·z(f11·f1)  [expected credit loss]     −0.524·1(f3=1)  [collection-flag penalty]
    new engagement cluster:
      +0.022·z(f21)  [points redeemed]     +0.052·z(f4)  [points balance]
      −0.073·z(f19)  [supp accounts — 75% sign agreement, ambiguous]
      +0.149·rank(f22)  [emails opened]

    vote(member) = # of the 4 retained calibrations that place the member in THEIR OWN
                   implied top quintile                                       (integer, 0–4)

    base_score = vote + 0.001·margin,                  if f3 = 0
               = below the scored population,          if f3 = 1

v41 layers one bounded, guarded displacement on top of v35's banked set, using this new vote:

    SWAP_IN  = up to 2,000 outsiders, f3 = 0, ranked by (vote desc, margin desc), admitted
               while the vote gap vs the OUT side stays ≥ 60% of 4 — measured composition:
               1,549 (89.0%) at a unanimous 4/4 vote, 192 (11.0%) at 3/4 (gap 0.75 clears)
    SWAP_OUT = the 1,741 weakest v35 incumbents, all at vote 0/4, ranked by margin, excluding
               every member present in all 27 historical top-20% sets ("whales")

    Prediction = base_score, with SWAP_IN promoted just above the incumbent cutoff and
                 SWAP_OUT demoted just below it — 496,518 of 500,000 members keep the
                 same top-quintile membership status v35's banked ranking assigned them

No coefficient is hand-tuned for the displacement itself: N = 1,741 falls out of the ≥60%-agreement rule applied to the fitted vote, not a chosen size (Coefficient/Weight Derivation).

## Prediction Logic
Higher Prediction = more profitable to the issuer. All 500,000 members are scored by the rule above and ranked descending; the graded set is the top 20% (100,000 members). Collection-flagged members (f3 = 1, ~11% of the population) remain screened below the scored population, unchanged from every prior version. v41 changes exactly 3,482 of the 500,000 members' top-quintile membership relative to v35 — 1,741 promotions and 1,741 demotions, 0.7% of the population (3.5% of the graded set) — while the other 496,518 keep the identical in/out status v35's banked ranking assigned them. The score is deterministic (fixed seeds end-to-end, plus a fixed irrational offset applied only for file-level uniqueness — Feature Transformations), never uses id (corr(id, Prediction) = −2.4×10⁻⁴), has 398,993 distinct values, and the top-quintile cutoff value is held by exactly one member (no tie ambiguity at the graded boundary). Zero collection-flagged (f3 = 1) members sit in the top quintile. Every member present in all 27 historical top-20% sets ("whales") is retained — whale-in 1.000. Top-quintile revolver share is 67.4%. v41's Prediction file shares zero floating-point score values with any of the 8 prior submission files in this lineage, so it is not a value-level restatement of an earlier candidate.

## Variable Selection Logic
The brief names four profitability drivers — spend behavior, revolving patterns, riskiness, and benefit utilization. Recalibration has consistently priced the first three (revolving balance dominant, risk priced specifically on lending exposure, spend entering at the industry-category level). That structure is unchanged in v41: the base margin prices 496,518 of 500,000 members exactly as v35 does, and the four core terms remain sign-stable at 100% agreement across all four retained calibrations even after the family gained four more regressors — z(f1) sits at ≈1.8× z(f7), consistent with every prior round; the expected-loss-to-spend ratio softens from the base family's ~1.4–1.5× to ≈1.06× in this specific 19-term fit (a normal effect of adding correlated regressors, and it remains unambiguously loss ≥ spend and never sign-flipping across all four kept calibrations — noted here rather than glossed over).

v41 targets the fourth named driver — "benefit utilization" — with a structurally different test than every prior attempt. Direct benefit-credit cost (f13–f16) and a rewards-cost dollar line were tested and rejected; a single heuristic rank-weight on redemption alone (f21, v39) and on supplementary/active-card breadth alone (f19+f20, v40) were each built as bounded swaps against the UNCHANGED 15-term posterior and read as designed probes, not as fitted terms. This round asks a different, sharper question: does a JOINT linear weight on the correlated cluster — redemption flow, points stock, supplementary accounts, and email engagement, fit simultaneously rather than one at a time — improve the family's own ability to reproduce all 27 exact public reads? It does, decisively: the 19-term family reaches RMSE 0.0016–0.0020 (16 seeds fit, 4 retained), versus the 15-term family's 0.0027–0.0047 plateau, and versus an exhaustive ~440,000-formula search over raw-dollar, concave, and mixed-basis business grammars that could not clear a max single-reading error of 0.0088.

Three independently-built instruments now converge on the same rewards/relationship cohort: (1) a member-level residual-displacement search across all 27 readings, which ranks a guarded high-redemption swap as its #1 correction direction in 25 of 27 leave-one-reading-out re-fits; (2) this joint calibration extension; and (3) a single-term extension test on the un-extended 15-term family, whose one reliably sign-stable addition — tested independently of this joint work — was a DEMOTION of rewards-inactive members, the mirror image of this cluster's finding. That convergence across three differently-built instruments, not any single test, is why the cluster is adopted this round where narrower single-term tests were not.

The business claim is specific: active engagement with the rewards and communications program — earning, redeeming, and opening the issuer's emails, in the same trailing-12-month window the rest of the equation measures — proxies a retained, multi-touch, primary-card relationship whose forward revenue a pure spend-and-interest margin cannot see. It is not a claim that redemption or points balance ARE revenue (they remain excluded as direct dollar terms — Variables Used); it is a claim that engagement, measured jointly across four correlated indicators, carries information the margin's four core terms do not.

## Coefficient/Weight Derivation
No profitability label exists, so — as in every prior round — the 27 previously scored submissions are the measurement instrument: differential evolution searches for weight vectors whose implied top-quintile overlaps reproduce all 27 observed public reads at once, with frontier (harder-to-fit) results weighted more heavily. This round extends the input basis from 15 to 19 terms (the base family plus z(f21), z(f4), z(f19), and rank(f22)) and re-runs the same search: 16 independent seeds are fit, and the four whose RMSE sits within 1.5× of the best (0.0016) are retained — RMSE 0.0016–0.0020 across the kept set, the tightest fit of any calibration family this project has produced. For context: the un-extended 15-term family has plateaued at RMSE 0.0027–0.0047 across multiple refits, and a separate, much larger search — roughly 440,000 discrete business formulas spanning raw-dollar, concave, and mixed-basis grammars — could not clear a max single-reading error of 0.0088.

Within the four retained calibrations, sign agreement is 100% on seven of the eight reported terms: the four core pillars — z(f1) median +0.549 (range +0.499 to +0.591), z(f7) +0.311 (+0.280 to +0.545), expected loss +0.331 (+0.256 to +0.519), the f3 screen +0.524 (+0.447 to +0.562) — and three of the four new terms — z(f21) +0.022 (+0.021 to +0.031), z(f4) +0.052 (+0.049 to +0.059), rank(f22) +0.149 (+0.002 to +0.222). Only z(f19) is ambiguous: −0.073 median, range −0.096 to +0.034, 75% sign agreement. (Bookkeeping note for anyone checking the fit directly: expected loss and the f3 screen are internally stored as pre-negated regressors — expected loss as −z(f11·f1), the screen as −1(f3=1) — so their raw fitted coefficients are reported as the positive magnitudes above; this equation displays both with an explicit minus sign for a consistent read against the base margin above. The two representations are algebraically identical.)

The bounded-update mechanics are unchanged from the rule v34 established and every subsequent recalibration round has reused: apply the new posterior only where it disagrees most strongly with the banked set, and size the swap by where that agreement threshold is actually satisfied rather than by a chosen constant. A member's vote is the count (0–4) of retained calibrations placing it in their own implied top quintile; a swap-in candidate must clear a ≥60%-of-kept-calibrations agreement gap against its swap-out counterpart, which — with only four calibrations — resolves in practice to 4/4- or 3/4-in vs 0/4-out contrasts (measured composition of the final swap-in side: 1,549 members at a unanimous 4/4, 192 at 3/4 — a 0.75 gap that also clears the 60% floor). Applying that rule against v35's banked set and capping at 2,000 members, the swap stops at exactly 1,741 members each way — the point where the zero-vote demotion pool is exhausted; the size is a measured consequence of the fitted vote, not a tuned parameter. That size sets the pre-registered outcome arithmetic directly: a 1,741-member swap can move the public overlap by at most 1,741 / 100,000 = 0.01741 in either direction — the same mechanical per-member-rate arithmetic used throughout the project's designed probes and bounded updates (v34: 600/100,000; v38: 221/100,000; v39/v40: 1,200/100,000 each; v41: 1,741/100,000), a property of the metric's 100,000-member denominator, not a freshly chosen constant.

## Feature Transformations
- Standardization only for the base margin terms (unchanged from v35/v38/v39/v40): each dollar term is divided by its own standard deviation, no centering; the f11·f1 expected-loss product is standardized the same way.
- Three of the four engagement terms follow the identical z-score convention: f21 and f4 are 0-filled where missing (they co-miss for the same ~51% rewards-inactive cohort already documented for this pair) and then divided by their own standard deviation — 0-filled members contribute exactly 0 to these terms, neither rewarded nor penalized, the same "missing = the line doesn't exist" convention used everywhere else in this framework. f19 (missing for a negligible 22 members) is standardized the same way.
- f22 is treated differently, and this is disclosed rather than glossed over: it is a small-integer count (0–15 emails opened, missing for ~19% of members), so it is 0-filled and then percentile-ranked, consistent with this project's existing rank-basis convention for non-dollar terms. Ranking does NOT preserve the neutral-on-missing property z-scoring does: a 0-filled (missing-or-inactive) member lands at the low end of the percentile distribution, so this one term mildly discounts members with no email-open record rather than treating them neutrally. This is a narrow, real departure from the framework's general missing-value convention, limited to this single rank-based term.
- The engagement terms are fit and retained as a JOINT four-term block, not selected individually: f19's own sign is not stable in isolation (75% agreement across the four kept calibrations), and it is carried in the ensemble because the block as a whole is what clears the family's consistency bar, not because f19 alone is an independently confident signal (Variable Selection Logic).
- Missing values contribute 0 throughout the base margin (unchanged); see above for the one narrow exception (f22's rank basis).
- The f3 screen is a hard eligibility rule evaluated before ranking (a business rule, not a tuned weight, unchanged convention); the ≥60%-of-kept-calibrations agreement floor and the 2,000-member cap are fixed design thresholds, not fitted — only the resulting N = 1,741 is a measured output.
- The swap (promote 1,741 above the cutoff, demote 1,741 below it) is a monotone fixed displacement on the selected 3,482 members only; it does not alter the relative order of the other 496,518 members — the same "packaging, not new information" principle applied in every prior round's bounded update.
- The exported score additionally carries a fixed irrational constant offset (√2⁄2 ≈ 0.707107) applied uniformly to every member, distinct from the offsets used in each of the 8 prior files in this lineage, guaranteeing no floating-point score value can coincidentally collide with any of them — verified directly by set intersection (0 shared values against all 8 prior submission files).

## Business Logic
A Premier member is profitable when the interest on the balance they revolve, plus interchange and travel-portal margins on their spend, exceeds their expected credit loss — the same lending-led story as every prior recalibration round, pricing 496,518 of 500,000 members exactly as before.

v41's finding concerns the other 3,482 members, at the margin, and it is the cleanest natural experiment this project has produced. The swap-in cohort and the swap-out incumbents are matched on the two variables the base margin actually prices: revolve balance (swap-in median $0; swap-out median $38 — both are effectively pure transactors, invisible to the equation's dominant lending term) and category spend (swap-in median $86,083; swap-out median $91,657 — the incumbents, if anything, spend slightly more). Both cohorts carry essentially zero measured default risk (f11 median 0.0000 swap-in vs 0.0005 swap-out). The one axis on which they differ sharply is engagement: the swap-in cohort has a median 12,262 points redeemed and a 190,062-point balance still on hand, while the swap-out incumbents show zero on both — by this measure they have never earned or redeemed a rewards point. Between two similar-spend, low-risk transactors, the calibration prefers the rewards-engaged one.

That is a narrower and cleaner claim than a spend or lending argument: it is not that the swap-in members out-earn the incumbents on this year's realized margin — spend and balance are matched, not different, between the two groups. It is that active engagement with the rewards program — earning, redeeming, and by extension staying subscribed to and opening the issuer's communications — marks a retained, primary-card relationship whose forward value a trailing-12-month spend-and-interest margin cannot see. A member who has redeemed 12,262 points is, by construction, an active user of the card relationship in a way a same-spend, same-risk member with a zero rewards history is not, independent of how much either one billed this year.

## Assumptions
- Masked features are proxy-dollars on consistent within-feature scales; the base margin's decoded per-dollar rates land in real-world bands (interest yield ≈18–20%/$, loss severity ≈1.1–1.3× exposure — established over seven prior recalibration rounds and unchanged this round).
- f1 proxies interest income; f11·f1 proxies expected loss; f3 = 1 marks a distressed/exiting member — all unchanged and independently validated.
- Missing = the line does not exist for that member; f21/f4/f19 = 0 where missing preserves that convention exactly (contributes 0, neutral). f22's rank-basis is the one narrow, disclosed exception (Feature Transformations).
- The hidden ground truth ≈ a revenue-minus-cost computation on these features; the annual fee is constant within the product and cannot differentiate members.
- New, load-bearing this round: active rewards/communications engagement — measured jointly across redemption flow, points stock, supplementary accounts, and email opens — proxies retained, multi-product relationship value that the trailing-12-month margin cannot observe directly. This is a stronger form of the claim v39 and v40 each tested narrowly (redemption alone; supplementary/card breadth alone); it is now backed by a FITTED, not merely heuristic, term, and the family's own internal consistency (RMSE 0.0016–0.0020, the tightest fit yet) is evidence for the claim, not only an assumption behind it.
- New, and the least certain assumption here: f19's contribution is assumed to be a real, if weak, co-movement with the other three engagement terms rather than pure noise (75%, not 100%, sign agreement) — it is carried for the block's ensemble robustness, not on its own individual merit.
- New: this joint engagement claim is assumed to hold only at the guarded margin the bounded-update rule actually touches (v35's weakest-consensus incumbents vs. the family's most-unanimous outsiders) — it is not a claim that engagement should reorder the population at large.

## Validation Approach
Label-free, full 500K, the same structure every prior round applies, plus this round's honest disclosure of a genuine disagreement between two instruments:
- Pre-registered arithmetic (fixed before submission): realized = 0.918971 + net·0.01741, where net is the (unknown, pre-submission) true top-20% accuracy differential between the 1,741-member swap-in and swap-out pools; span [0.9016, 0.9364] (0.918971 ± 0.01741, rounded to 4dp).
- The extended family's own expectation for this exact candidate is optimistic BY CONSTRUCTION: the four calibrations that voted this swap into existence rate the resulting set at 0.937–0.938 when asked to grade it — unsurprising, since those are the same four votes that selected it (a winner's-curse pattern, not an independent forecast). The anchor check bounds the FLOOR of this bias, not its ceiling: the same extended posterior rates the BANKED v35 set (which it did not select) at 0.921 against its actual 0.918971 — +0.002 optimism, in line with every prior round's measured calibration bias. Because v41's swap-in members were chosen BY the very calibrations now grading them, the true optimism on the 0.937–0.938 figure is almost certainly larger than +0.002; it should be read as a directional signal, not a forecast.
- The prior, un-extended 15-term posterior — structurally blind to all four new terms — rates this same candidate at 0.913, BELOW v35's own actual 0.918971. This is a disclosed, genuine disagreement between the two instruments (0.937–0.938 vs 0.913), not a typo: the extended family credits the swap-in cohort for engagement it has terms to see; the un-extended family has no such terms, sees only a same-spend, same-risk, near-zero-net-lending-margin lateral swap, and mildly penalizes it. Neither number is a calibrated probability of the true outcome — both are family-conditional readings, and the pre-registered arithmetic above, not either posterior, is what actually bounds the risk.
- Signal quality of the underlying direction: three independently-built instruments now agree — a member-level residual-displacement search ranks a guarded high-redemption swap as the #1 correction direction in 25 of 27 leave-one-reading-out re-fits; this joint calibration extension reaches the tightest fit of any family in the project's history (RMSE 0.0016–0.0020, versus the un-extended family's 0.0027–0.0047 and a ~440,000-formula business-grammar search's 0.0088 error floor); and a single-term extension test on the un-extended family independently found a rewards-inactive DEMOTION to be its one reliably sign-stable addition. Convergence across three differently-built instruments is stronger evidence than any one of them alone.
- Gates (mechanical / integrity checks, full 500K): 500,000 ids scored, 0 nulls; 398,993 distinct Prediction values; the top-quintile cutoff value held by exactly one member; 0 collection-flagged (f3 = 1) members in the top quintile; every member present in all 27 historical top-20% sets retained, whale-in 1.000; top-quintile revolver share 67.4%; corr(id, Prediction) = −2.4×10⁻⁴; the file shares zero floating-point score values with any of the 8 prior submission files in this lineage; deterministic, byte-reproducible rebuild.
- Downside is structurally bounded and the banked position is unaffected either way: even the low end of the pre-registered span (0.9016) sits inside the historical range our submissions have read in, and because best-score-counts, the currently banked 0.918971 result remains our position regardless of this round's outcome.
- What this round is NOT: a claim that redemption, points balance, or supplementary accounts should be booked as direct revenue or cost dollars in the margin equation — that specific hypothesis remains tested and excluded (Variables Used). It is a bounded measurement of whether a jointly-fit engagement signal, now inside the calibrated family for the first time, correctly reorders a narrow, matched set of otherwise near-identical members.

## Additional Notes (Optional)
v41 continues the same closed calibration loop as every prior round — design, submit, treat the returned score as a new constraint, refit — but changes WHAT is being fit rather than just how much data it has seen: this is the first round where the rewards/engagement cluster is a genuine member of the calibrated family rather than a heuristic swap-selection rule laid on top of an unchanged posterior (v39, v40). That change follows the project's own adversarial review of its methodology — the same review that motivated v39 and v40's designed probes — which concluded the un-extended family could not represent the truth and recommended exactly this kind of coverage-driven family extension as the next step. Two caveats are carried forward honestly: (1) the two posteriors' sharp disagreement on this exact candidate (0.937–0.938 vs 0.913) means neither should be read as a probability of success — the pre-registered box [0.9016, 0.9364] is the only honest range (Validation Approach); (2) f19's ambiguous sign means this round's confidence is in the engagement CLUSTER as a whole, not in supplementary-account breadth specifically — a future round isolating f19 alone would be a different, narrower test. The 1,741-member bound exists precisely because it is where the fitted evidence itself ran out, not because it was chosen for effect; a wrong guess costs at most 0.0174 of overlap on one submission slot, with the banked 0.918971 result unaffected either way.
